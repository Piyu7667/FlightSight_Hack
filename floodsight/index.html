When the roads are gone, the satellite still sees.

FloodSight turns one radar pass into a rescue map: what the water destroyed, which villages are cut off, and where help should go first.

Open the live demo Built for the IIT Mandi Hackathon, Track B: Mapping Flood Damage from Space.

Rescuers are blind at the worst moment

Ground reports stop. Phone lines fail with the first surge, so the people who most need help are the ones who can no longer ask for it.

Damage is invisible. No one knows which bridges still stand or which roads are under water until a team drives there and turns back.

Hours are lost. Cloud cover blocks ordinary satellite photos, so aid goes to the villages that are easiest to see, not the ones most cut off.

From one satellite pass to a ranked rescue list

  1. Capture. Sentinel-1 radar sees through clouds and night. We compare a pre-flood and a during-flood image.
  2. Find the water. Pixels that turned dark become a flood mask, using change detection or a U-Net trained on Sen1Floods11.
  3. Count the damage. The mask is laid over OpenStreetMap roads and bridges to mark each one open or lost.
  4. Find who is cut off. A road graph with the lost links removed shows which villages can no longer reach a hospital or camp.
  5. Rank and deliver. Villages are ranked by population and vulnerability, then exported as an offline map for field teams.

    What we build next

    Stack

    Python, FastAPI, Rasterio, GeoPandas, OSMnx and NetworkX for analysis. PyTorch U-Net for water segmentation. MapLibre front end with offline GeoJSON and PDF export. Data: Sentinel-1/2, SRTM DEM, OpenStreetMap, WorldPop, ISRO Bhuvan.

    Roadmap

    Now: simulated district and working isolation logic. Next: replay a real flood such as Kerala 2018 end to end. Later: DEM-based spread forecasts and alerts to district teams, then cyclones and landslides.
